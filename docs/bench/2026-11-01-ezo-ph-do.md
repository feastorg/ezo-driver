# 2026-11-01: pH and DO circuits over Linux I2C

**Status: complete.** This is the first run of ezo-driver against real circuits.

- **Passed:** identity, status, LED, readings, and the NOT_READY, NO_DATA and transport errors. Also a 3-point pH calibration, a 2-point DO calibration, and Sleep and wake on DO firmware 2.17.
- **Failed:** Sleep on the pH circuit, firmware 2.15. It never woke, and only a power cycle brought it back (#33).
- **New issues from this session:** #32, #33 and #34.

**Verifies:** 0.8.0 from PyPI, including:

- status-specific read errors (#23, released in 0.7.0);
- errno, bus and address on `EzoTransportError` (#29, released in 0.8.0).

**By:** @CameronBrooks11, 2026-11-01 to 2026-11-02.

## Notes

- **Sleep on pH firmware 2.15.**
  - The vendor's pH firmware changelog lists 2.16 as "Fixed bug in I2C mode with timing and sleep mode". The DO circuit, on 2.17, after the DO sleep fixes, woke normally (E11).
  - That 2.16 fixes the pH failure is inferred from the changelog entry, not tested on a pH circuit.
  - #33 covers documenting this; #34 covers a firmware-version lookup for callers.
- **pH slope.**
  - Base 68.9 % and a -27.9 mV offset are outside the vendor's typical figures for a new probe: above 95 %, and within about ±5 mV.
  - The pH 10 bottle was fresh, so the probe is the more likely cause. Cleaning the probe and recalibrating would tell.
  - **2026-11-04 follow-up:** the probe was cleaned and recalibrated with fresh pH 7, 4 and 10 buffers, each read until steady.
    - The slope came out at acid 99.8 %, base 97.5 %, neutral -28.56 mV.
    - Cleaning brought both slopes back above 95 %.
    - The offset hardly moved with a fresh pH 7 bottle. So it belongs to the probe (most likely its reference side or its age), not to the buffer, and the calibration corrects for it. The datasheet puts noticeable performance issues beyond ±10 mV, so watch whether it grows.
  - **Datasheet V6.2 (10/26):** it does not print the 95 %, ±5 mV or ±10 mV figures above. It expects a new probe above 90 %, with the two slopes similar, and an offset of about 15 mV or lower. Against those, the first calibration (base 68.9 %, -27.9 mV) is still outside on both. After cleaning, the slopes (99.8 %, 97.5 %) are inside and the offset (-28.56 mV) is still outside.
- **DO after the air calibration.** It rose from 9.13 to 9.48 mg/L within 8 minutes, after about 18 h in zero solution. Check it in air again, and repeat `Cal` once it holds steady.
- **Decimal parsing.** Readings such as `6.079000000000001` and `0.6800000000000002` come from the C parser, not the circuit (#32).
